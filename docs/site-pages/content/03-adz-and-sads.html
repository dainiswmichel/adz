Two kinds of advertising can appear in the same slot on the same page, and they are not the same thing at all. It helps to have separate words for them.

A sad

A sad is a surveillance-based ad. The network is handed a slot and a publisher identifier, and it decides for itself what to show from data it has gathered about the person looking at the screen. The publisher does not choose the ad. The reader certainly does not. You were served a sad; you were served sads.

This is the overwhelming majority of advertising on the web, and it is not inherently fraudulent or malicious. It is simply advertising chosen by inference from observation rather than by anybody asking.

An adzad

An adzad is an ad that answers something the reader actually said. The reader's own words reach the thing serving the ad, and what comes back addresses them. The plural is adz, and the z is the part that means refined.

An adzad is only possible where the provider accepts a query. Some do: a search-based affiliate link takes the reader's words and returns results for them. Some do not: a display network takes an account identifier and nothing else.

Why the distinction matters to a publisher

A publisher deciding what to run can ask one question of any provider: can I send this the reader's own request?

If yes, that provider can serve adz, and the reader gets something they asked for. If no, the provider can only serve sads, which may still be worth running, but should be understood for what it is.

That question is answerable by looking, and it does not depend on anyone's marketing. It is also the only distinction that changes what the reader experiences.

Running both

There is no requirement to choose. A rotation can carry a network unit in one slot and an invitation in the next. A reader who writes nothing sees the network units a site already ran. A reader who writes something gets adz as well.

That is deliberate. A publisher should not have to gamble their income on a principle, and a reader should not have to opt into anything to read an article.
