If you run a site and you are weighing this up, here is what adopting permission-based advertising actually involves, including the parts that are inconvenient.

You keep what you already run

The plugin does not replace your existing advertising. Your current networks stay connected and keep paying. Permission-based slots are added into the same rotation alongside them, and you decide the proportion. If one adz in ten is permission-based, that is a valid configuration, and so is none.

This matters because the alternative — asking a publisher to switch off their income on principle — is not a serious proposal.

What you actually set up

  1. Connect the networks and affiliate programmes you already have accounts with. Each one needs its own identifiers, which the provider gives you.
  2. Build a rotation: an invitation panel, plus whatever mix of your own adz and provider units you want.
  3. Place that rotation with a shortcode carrying only its name.

Everything else — the format, the size, the order — is configured once on the rotation and applies everywhere it appears.

Your own adz

A site owner can create adz themselves: an image and a link is a complete one. These cost no revenue share and involve no third party, and they are the only creative you fully control. They are also the best demonstration of the idea, because you can write them to answer what your readers actually ask for.

The honest limitations

  • Readers have to opt in. Someone who writes nothing sees only what you were already serving. Take-up is not guaranteed and is not something a plugin can manufacture.
  • Not every provider accepts a query. Display networks take an account identifier and decide for themselves. Only search-based affiliate links can be sent a reader's own words.
  • Affiliate income is settled, not immediate. A commission arrives when a purchase completes and clears, which is a different rhythm from per-impression revenue.
  • It is more setup than a single script tag. Each provider needs its own identifiers entered correctly.

What you can tell your readers

This is the part that is genuinely different. You can say, accurately, that a reader who uses the invitation panel is not profiled, not followed between sites, and not identified; that what they typed stays in their own browser; and that they can delete it at any moment and the advertising stops.

Very few publishers can say anything of the kind about the advertising they carry.
